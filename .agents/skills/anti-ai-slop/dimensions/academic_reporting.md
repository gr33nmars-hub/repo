# Dimension Review: Academic, Analytical, and Professional Reporting

Use for academic writing, research summaries, policy memos, strategy documents, consulting reports, executive briefs, and analytical deliverables.

## Purpose

Reports should support a judgment. They need credible evidence, appropriate uncertainty, and explicit implications.

## Common generic patterns

| Pattern | Visible sign | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Formal abstraction | “pivotal,” “intricate,” “multifaceted,” “robust,” “comprehensive,” “realm” | Inflates tone without adding evidence |
| Citation theater | “Studies show,” “research suggests,” or exact statistics without sources | Reduces trust |
| Balanced paralysis | Equal pros and cons with no recommendation | Fails decision support |
| Passive neutrality | “It may be argued that...” | Hides author responsibility |
| Template conclusion | “Further research is needed” | Avoids practical implication |
| Pseudo-framework | 2x2 matrices or stages without analytical necessity | Looks strategic but may not explain causality |
| Source integrity smell | Fake DOI/URL, irrelevant citation, stale evidence, invented page number, unverifiable quote | Creates false authority |
| Benchmark without denominator | Precise metric but no sample, period, scope, or definition | Makes comparison impossible |

## Review checklist

- What decision, hypothesis, or question does the report answer?
- Are claims matched with evidence strength?
- Are sources named, dated, and scoped?
- Are assumptions separated from findings?
- Is uncertainty quantified or explained where possible?
- Does the report make a recommendation or state why no recommendation is possible?
- Are tradeoffs ranked rather than merely listed?

## Reporting repair patterns

### Add a recommendation

```text
Weak: Option A and Option B each present advantages and limitations.
Better: Choose Option B for the first rollout because it reduces implementation risk. Revisit Option A only if usage exceeds the forecast by 40% or more.
```

### Qualify claims

```text
Weak: This approach significantly improves retention.
Better: This approach is expected to improve short-term retention for new users, but the estimate should be treated as provisional until cohort data is available.
```

### Replace style words

| Style-heavy phrase | More useful alternative |
|---|---|
| “a pivotal factor” | “the constraint that determines whether the plan works” |
| “a multifaceted challenge” | “three separate problems: cost, governance, and adoption” |
| “underscores the importance of” | “changes the priority from X to Y” |

## Evidence gate

For every number, quote, benchmark, or comparison, check:

- source;
- date;
- scope;
- method;
- definition;
- relevance to the current case.

Remove or qualify claims that cannot pass this gate.

## Source integrity checks

| Smell | Review action |
|---|---|
| DOI, URL, or citation cannot be verified | Remove it or mark it as needing source verification. |
| Source exists but does not support the claim | Rewrite the claim to match the source or replace the source. |
| Quote has no page, timestamp, or traceable location | Add location or paraphrase with a source note. |
| Evidence is stale for a changing field | Add date, scope, and currentness warning. |
| Benchmark lacks denominator or definition | Define metric, sample, time period, and comparison basis. |

## Acceptance criteria

- The report has a clear question or decision.
- Findings are separated from assumptions and recommendations.
- Important claims are sourced or qualified.
- The conclusion contains a decision, implication, or test plan.
- Formal wording does not substitute for evidence.
