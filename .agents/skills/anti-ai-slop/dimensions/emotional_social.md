# Dimension Review: Emotional, Essay, and Social Writing

Use for essays, social media posts, founder notes, personal reflections, speeches, thought-leadership posts, and narrative content.

## Purpose

Emotional writing should create recognition, tension, memory, or trust. It should not only sound profound.

## Common generic patterns

| Pattern | Visible sign | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Aphoristic compression | “X is not Y. It is Z.” | Sounds meaningful but may be empty |
| Repeated short sentences | “I stopped. I thought. I chose.” | Creates artificial drama |
| Polished vulnerability | Confession without specific detail, cost, or contradiction | Feels manufactured |
| Lesson ending | “In the end, what matters is...” | Reduces story to moral instruction |
| Abstract emotion | “fear,” “growth,” “resilience,” “journey” without scene | Reader cannot see or verify it |
| Symmetric contrast | “Not failure, but feedback” | Familiar motivational rhythm |

## Review checklist

- Is there a scene, object, person, place, date, or sensory detail?
- What tension remains unresolved?
- What did the speaker risk, lose, misunderstand, or change?
- Is the ending earned by the story?
- Could this post be attributed to many people without changing details?
- Does every short sentence earn its emphasis?

## Rewrite patterns

### Add scene before lesson

```text
Weak: Leadership is not about having all the answers.
Better: The room went quiet after the second missed deadline. I had a slide with answers, but the team needed me to name the tradeoff we had been avoiding.
```

### Replace abstraction with detail

| Abstract | Grounded |
|---|---|
| “I learned resilience” | “I sent the third apology email before 8 a.m.” |
| “We faced uncertainty” | “The contract renewal was due Friday, and the dashboard still showed conflicting numbers” |
| “It changed how I think” | “I stopped asking for updates and started asking what decision the update supported” |

### Fix the ending

| Weak ending | Better direction |
|---|---|
| Moral | Consequence |
| Inspirational quote | Concrete decision |
| Summary of lesson | Image or unresolved tension |

## Acceptance criteria

- The piece contains concrete detail that could only come from the author or context.
- The emotional claim is supported by a scene or consequence.
- Aphorisms are limited and earned.
- The ending does not flatten the story into a generic lesson.
