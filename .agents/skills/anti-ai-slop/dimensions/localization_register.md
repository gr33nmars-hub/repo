# Dimension Review: Localization and Register

Use for translated copy, multilingual UI, regional reports, international marketing, support replies, and culturally specific visuals or examples.

## Purpose

Localized output should sound native to the intended audience and respect local conventions. It should not read like source-language syntax with translated words.

## Common generic / AI-smell patterns

| Pattern | Visible sign | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Translationese | Source-language sentence order, literal idioms, unnatural connectors | Feels imported and lowers trust |
| Register mismatch | Formal and casual forms mixed, wrong honorific level, inconsistent politeness | Can sound rude, childish, or corporate in the wrong way |
| Locale-format error | Wrong date, currency, unit, address, name, phone, or number format | Creates usability and credibility issues |
| Cultural genericness | “Global audience” language with no local example, institution, or constraint | Avoids the real audience |
| Mixed terminology | Same product, feature, or legal term translated several ways | Confuses users and reviewers |
| Compliance mismatch | References laws, consent, age, tax, or privacy rules from the wrong region | Creates legal and trust risk |

## Review checklist

- What locale, language variant, and formality level is expected?
- Are dates, currency, units, addresses, names, phone numbers, and examples localized?
- Are UI labels short, idiomatic, and consistent?
- Are product terms, legal terms, and support phrases translated consistently?
- Does bilingual copy keep equivalent meaning and tone across languages?
- Are local compliance or cultural references accurate and scoped?

## Repair patterns

| Weak pattern | Better direction |
|---|---|
| Literal translation of a slogan | Rewrite around the local buyer’s task and idiom. |
| Mixed formal/casual support reply | Choose one register and apply it consistently. |
| US date/currency in Korean copy | Convert to the target locale format or explain why US format is intentional. |
| Generic global example | Use a local workflow, institution, regulation, or user scenario. |
| Inconsistent feature term | Create a glossary entry and apply it across UI/copy. |

## Acceptance criteria

- Locale and register are explicit.
- Formatting conventions match the audience.
- Key terms are consistent.
- Local examples are accurate or clearly omitted.
- Translation does not preserve source-language awkwardness.
