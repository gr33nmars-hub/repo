# Dimension Review: Marketing, Brand, and Sales Copy

Use for landing pages, ads, product pages, brand messaging, sales collateral, emails, launch posts, and pitch copy.

## Purpose

Marketing copy should make a specific audience understand a specific value proposition and take a specific next step.

## Common generic patterns

| Pattern | Visible sign | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract transformation | “Unlock,” “supercharge,” “reimagine,” “transform,” “empower” | Sounds energetic but does not explain the product |
| Empty modernity | “for modern teams,” “next-generation,” “future of X” | Substitutes category aesthetics for positioning |
| Formulaic contrast | “Not just a tool, but a platform” | Familiar rhythm with low information |
| Seamless promise | “seamless,” “frictionless,” “effortless” without mechanism | Creates unsupported expectation |
| Vague social proof | “trusted by teams worldwide” | Weakens credibility when not backed by names or numbers |
| Generic CTA | “Get started,” “Learn more,” “Book a demo” repeated everywhere | Does not match buyer intent or funnel stage |
| Compliance/security theater | Trust badges, “secure by design,” SOC 2/HIPAA/GDPR claims without scope or proof | Overpromises trust and creates review risk |
| SEO content-farm copy | Keyword repetition, generic FAQ block, “ultimate guide” filler, duplicate H2 intent | Dilutes positioning and reader trust |

## Review checklist

- Who exactly is the buyer, user, evaluator, or skeptic?
- What painful workflow, cost, risk, or delay is being solved?
- What does the product take as input?
- What does it produce as output?
- What is the mechanism that makes the result possible?
- What proof supports the promise?
- What objection is most likely, and does the copy answer it?
- Is the CTA matched to the user’s readiness?
- Are trust, privacy, compliance, or security claims scoped and verifiable?
- Are SEO sections written for a real buyer question, not only a keyword target?

## Copy repair patterns

### Use the value proposition formula

```text
For [specific user],
who struggles with [specific workflow/problem],
[product] turns [input]
into [specific output]
so they can [measurable or observable outcome]
without [major friction or risk].
```

### Replace generic SaaS language

| Generic | Better |
|---|---|
| “Supercharge your workflow” | “Convert support calls into coachable QA notes before the next shift starts” |
| “Streamline collaboration” | “Keep product decisions attached to the customer quote that caused them” |
| “Unlock insights” | “Find which churned accounts mentioned billing confusion in the last 30 days” |
| “All-in-one platform” | “One queue for intake, review, approval, and client delivery” |

### Fix CTAs

| Funnel stage | Better CTA direction |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Show example, view workflow, compare current process |
| Evaluation | See integration list, calculate savings, inspect security model |
| Purchase | Book implementation call, start pilot, request procurement packet |
| Expansion | Invite team, connect data source, create first project |

## Brand-fit review

Ask:

- Could a competitor use the same copy unchanged?
- Does the vocabulary match the company’s category and buyer maturity?
- Are claims consistent with product reality?
- Is the tone too inflated for the evidence?
- Does the copy contain at least one proprietary phrase, workflow, or point of view?

## Trust-claim review

For security, privacy, compliance, and certification claims:

- name the certification, control, or policy;
- add scope and date where relevant;
- avoid badges or seals that are decorative or unsupported;
- separate “planned,” “in progress,” and “available now” claims;
- route high-stakes claims to legal/security review before delivery.

## Acceptance criteria

- The copy names the user, pain, mechanism, and outcome.
- Generic verbs are replaced with domain-specific verbs.
- Proof is specific or removed.
- CTA matches the user’s stage.
- The page cannot be easily rebranded for a different product without rewriting core sections.
