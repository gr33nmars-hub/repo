# Dimension Review: Presentation Decks

Use for pitch decks, strategy decks, training decks, research presentations, lecture materials, and executive slide documents.

## Purpose

A deck should support spoken delivery, decision-making, or independent reading. Each slide needs one job.

## Common generic patterns

| Pattern | Visible sign | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Three-bullet rhythm | Every slide has a title and three bullets | Predictable but not memorable |
| Placeholder icons | Same icon style across unrelated ideas | Adds decoration without meaning |
| Glossy 3D visuals | Floating objects, robots, spheres, glass blocks | Looks polished but may not explain anything |
| Unsupported metrics | Precise numbers without source/date/definition | Trust risk |
| Generic journey | Problem → solution → benefits → next steps regardless of content | Ignores actual argument |
| Weak close | “Thank you” or “Questions?” only | No decision, ask, or next step |

## Review checklist

- What is the deck type: pitch, decision, education, report, or sales?
- Does each slide have one message, not just one topic?
- Are slide titles claims rather than labels?
- Is evidence visible where decisions are requested?
- Are charts readable and sourced?
- Does visual hierarchy guide the eye in the intended order?
- Does the ending include the ask, decision, or next action?

## Slide-level review

For each slide, check:

```text
Slide job:
Main message:
Audience action:
Evidence:
Visual support:
Remove / revise:
```

## Repair patterns

| Weak slide title | Better claim title |
|---|---|
| “Market Opportunity” | “Mid-market teams now have budget for automated QA, but implementation trust is the blocker” |
| “Our Solution” | “We reduce review time by turning every call into a structured coaching note” |
| “Benefits” | “The first measurable win is manager review time, not agent productivity” |
| “Next Steps” | “Approve a 30-day pilot with 3 teams and one integration” |

## Visual repair

- Replace generic icons with screenshots, diagrams, examples, or customer quotes.
- Use one chart per key claim.
- Remove decorative 3D unless it acts as a memory device.
- Keep layout rhythm varied enough to match argument importance.
- Use speaker notes for nuance; do not overload the slide.

## Evidence requirements

For every metric:

- define the metric;
- add source or method;
- add time period;
- identify whether it is actual, estimate, benchmark, or target.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every slide has a clear job.
- Titles make claims where appropriate.
- Visuals clarify, prove, or make memorable.
- Generic icons and decorative images are reduced.
- The final slide states the decision, ask, or next step.
